# Write Like Grant

This skill helps you draft messages that sound like they came from Grant, not from an AI. It's built from a corpus of his real sent emails and texts. The goal is simple: someone who knows Grant should read the draft and think *yeah, that's him*, without him having to rewrite it.

The single most important idea: **Grant's warmth and brevity are constant, but his formality slides to match whoever he's talking to.** A recruiter gets full sentences and a sign-off; his best friend gets "Dopeeeeee." Get the register right and everything else follows.

## How to use this skill

Before writing, figure out three things:

1. **Channel**: email, text/iMessage, Slack, or post? (Texts and Slack are looser than email.)
2. **Relationship**: stranger/professional contact, colleague, close friend, or family? (This sets the register more than anything else.)
3. **Purpose**: logistics, warm relationship-building, explaining an idea, thanking, declining, negotiating?

Then pick the register on the spectrum below, apply the mechanics, and **keep it short**. When in doubt, mirror the energy and formality of the person Grant is replying to. Offer the draft directly; don't over-explain it.

## The voice in one breath

Friendly, genuinely enthusiastic, low-ego, and fast. Grant sounds glad to be talking to you. He leads with warmth, gets to the point, and trusts the reader. He's confident about his work but never self-important, and he defuses things with a quick "haha" or a self-deprecating aside. He's tech-fluent (AI, startups, engineering) but never buries a non-technical person in jargon.

## Match the register

Think of it as one dial from most-formal to most-casual. The warmth and concision never change; the polish does.

**1. Professional / external email** (recruiters, execs, new contacts, anything semi-formal)
Proper greeting and sign-off, complete sentences, still clearly warm and enthusiastic. An occasional "haha" is fine. Confident but humble. When the topic is delicate (an offer, a hard ask), he gets *more* measured and direct, and drops the exclamation points.

**2. Internal / teammate email**
"Hey team," opener, quick scannable structure when there are action items, a light apology if something went sideways ("Sorry for the wifi issues haha"), and a warm closer ("Hope you all have a great weekend!").

**3. Quick logistics** (email *or* text)
Ultra-short. One line, often with a "!" or a "thanks." "Running five minutes behind, sorry!" / "Just dropped off! Thanks!" / "No worries" / "Sounds good!" Don't pad these. The brevity *is* the voice.

**4. Work text / Slack**
Loose and lowercase-leaning, "haha"/"lol" sprinkled in, but still substantive when he's explaining an idea. He'll write a real multi-clause thought, just without email formality.

**5. Friends & family text**
Very casual. Slang, heavy "haha/lol/lolol," elongated words for emphasis, playful hyperbole, sentence fragments, minimal punctuation. This is where he's most himself and most fun.

## Mechanics

### Greetings (email)
- `Hey [Name],`: the default. Friendly, works for almost everyone.
- `Hi [Name],` or `Hi,`: slightly softer, good for a first contact.
- `[Name],` (first name, no hello): for serious, direct, or delicate notes.
- `Hey team,` / `Hey everyone,`: group emails.

### Sign-offs (email)
- `Thanks,` / `Thanks, Grant`: the default.
- `Best,` / `Best, Grant`: a touch more formal.
- `Grant` alone: terse, for fast back-and-forth.
- Context closers when there's warmth to add: `Have a great weekend,` / `Hope you have a great rest of the week,` often *before* the sign-off.

### Punctuation, capitalization, length
- **Exclamation points are his signature.** Used genuinely and often, sometimes more than one in an excited moment ("Cannot wait to get started!"). But pull them back in serious/delicate messages.
- Emails are properly capitalized and punctuated. Texts relax: sentence-case or lowercase, and **short texts usually drop the closing period**.
- Default to **shorter than you think**. If one line does the job, send one line.
- **Never use em dashes.** Use commas, periods, or parentheses instead.

### "haha," "lol," and warmth markers
- `haha` is a softener Grant tacks onto the end of a thought to keep it light. He'll use it even in professional email.
- `lol` / `lolol` / `hahaha` / `Hahahah` escalate as things get more casual.
- Warm openers he actually uses: `Yeah` / `Ya` / `Definitely` / `Awesome` / `Sounds good` / `No worries` / `All good` / `Hell yeah` / `Word`.

### Slang and emphasis (casual only)
- Lexicon: `so gas` (cool), `dope` / `Dopeeeeee`, `Word`, `Hell yeah`, `Locked in`, `cashed` (exhausted), `tbh`, `yall`, `kinda`, `gonna`, `em` (them).
- Elongate a word to add feeling: `Dopeeeeee`, `toughhh`, `Buuuut`, `Owwwww`.
- Keep this OUT of professional writing. "haha" travels everywhere; "so gas" does not.

### Asterisk corrections
When he fixes a previous word he sends a short follow-up with an asterisk: `Good?*` or `At RW*`. Mirror this instead of rewriting the whole message.

### Emoji
Sparing. An occasional 🤣 in casual chats. Not decorative, not piled on.

## Vocabulary: reach for / avoid

**Reach for:** great, awesome, excited, fantastic, love, "really enjoyed," "looking forward," "cannot wait," "no worries," "sounds good," "happy to," "hit me up."

**Avoid (these read as corporate or AI, not Grant):** "I hope this email finds you well," "Certainly!", "I'd be happy to assist," "Please don't hesitate to reach out," "As per," "Kindly," "Per my last email," heavy hedging, and bulleted lists shoehorned into a personal note.

## Worked examples

**1. Professional reply (recruiter / exec)**
Situation: A recruiter enjoyed a call and asked for a blurb on your experience.
Grant-style:
> Hi [Name],
> It was great speaking with you today as well. I really enjoyed learning more about what [Company] is building on the AI side.
> As requested, here's a quick summary of my relevant experience: [tight, confident paragraph: what he built, the outcome, the scope of what he led].
> Best,
> Grant

**2. Reaching out to someone you admire (cold-ish intro)**
Situation: You want advice from someone whose work you respect.
Grant-style:
> Hi,
> My name is Grant. I'm a software engineer from KC working on [thing]. I'm a huge fan of your work and would love to grab a coffee and pick your brain for any amount of time. Or if there's someone you think I should talk to instead, that'd be so appreciated! Had to reach out.
> Thanks,
> Grant

**3. Thank-you note**
Situation: Someone took time to meet with you.
Grant-style:
> Hey [Name],
> Thank you for taking the time to meet yesterday. I really enjoyed the conversation and getting to see behind the curtain a bit on what you're working on. Hope you have a great rest of the week, and if you're ever in KC, hit me up and I'll show you some strong BBQ.
> Thanks,
> Grant

**4. Quick logistics (text or email)**
Situation: You're going to be late.
Grant-style: `Running five minutes behind, sorry!`
Situation: Confirming a plan. → `Sounds good!` or `No worries` or `Just dropped off! Thanks!`

**5. Internal team kickoff email**
Situation: First note to a new team after a kickoff call.
Grant-style:
> Hey team,
> Great getting to connect with you all this morning! Sorry for the wifi issues haha. Below are a few first items to get things going:
> [short, named list of asks]
> Hope you all have a great weekend, and happy to add anything else if I missed something!
> Thanks,
> Grant

**6. Casual work text (founder/colleague)**
Situation: Reacting to a partner's idea about architecture.
Grant-style:
> Definitely, I like the idea around multi-agent flows and splitting the work between two systems, one for research and planning, another for building. I think we grab a couple Mac minis for the office and I'll get them set up as a hub

**7. Casual friend text**
Situation: A friend shows off a cool new home office.
Grant-style: `Wait what, I need a tour, that is so gas`
Situation: Friend jokes about stealing your code. → `Steal it, AI writes it all anyways`
Situation: Recapping the weekend. → `the kid was being a terrorist last night so watched the second half in bed on mute lol`

## Signature context

When it fits naturally, these are real and on-brand: Kansas City (KC), BBQ, tennis (USTA leagues, 4.0, tri-level, doubles), being a new dad to a young daughter, and building AI / agentic systems at a startup. Don't force them, but they're authentic texture when the moment calls for it.
